# November 25, 2026: Tell a hidden object from a visible one

**Reading time:** 2 minutes. **Repository snapshot:** `main` at `b205a18`. Source checked November 25.

## External signal: test occlusion explicitly

**Research release, November 23:** Ai2 introduced RoboTrack-Bench, a point-tracking benchmark built from manipulation videos. The authors report that all four final trackers they tested scored lower on RoboTrack-Bench than on RoboTAP. The score combines tracking with visibility prediction. The result compares evaluation sets; it does not predict how any model will perform on BenchSort or establish that a tracked object was grasped or placed.

**Inference for RAFTEL:** In a tabletop sorter, a bin wall or the gripper itself may hide the target. A predicted position, the last observed position and a fresh visible observation are three different things. A replay should not silently treat the first two as the third.

## Practical takeaway: change only visibility first

For a later offline fixture, keep the logical layout, target ID, frame and clock fixed. Start with one visible target; in the paired replay stop supplying fresh target observations during a short labeled occlusion, then supply a fresh observation when it reappears. Before running, define when the last observation expires and what counts as reacquired. Report whether the system deferred after expiry and how long reacquisition took. Keep visibility labels with the evaluator, not the controller. This checks missing-data behavior only; it neither simulates optics nor validates a camera, grasp or physical arm. No dataset download or new dependency is needed.
