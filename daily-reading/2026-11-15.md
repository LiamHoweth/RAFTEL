## Read first: a measurement record is not qualification

The [cleaned-mass metadata guide](../arm-01/docs/thor-measurement-readiness.md) is useful background for the next fabrication evidence. It builds on the existing [fabrication-record validator](../arm-01/src/raftel_arm01/engineering/fabrication_records.py): slicer model mass, support/purge waste, cleaned measured mass and synthetic examples remain separate. Unknown values stay unknown.

The companion records uncertainty declarations, calibration windows and supporting-file identity. Passing it means the metadata is available for engineering review, not that an instrument, part or mass model has been physically qualified. No new measuring equipment or software dependency is implied.

## Company reading: ASUS starts with a fixed workstation

No materially relevant new November 14–15 primary announcement was found in today's search. This **November 13** [ASUS Gallop post](https://press.asus.com/blog/factory-proven-physical-ai-asus-gallop-humanoid-robot) is an older, newly selected reading, not today's launch news.

ASUS describes two arms on a fixed base, initially packing 15 motherboard accessories, many in transparent bags. **99.5% success within 20 seconds is its target**, not a reported achieved result. The company describes fixed interfaces between planning, supervision, skills and control, with customer pilots planned for 2027.

The post gives no trial denominator or achieved full-shift reliability. Its title and factory setting do not establish those results, affordability or household robustness.

**RAFTEL relevance:** keep the stationary BenchSort scope and explicit interfaces. Borrow the idea of a whole-job acceptance criterion, while retaining our own [verification plan](../arm-01/docs/verification-plan.md) and [phased gates](../docs/roadmap.md). Gallop is a company comparison, not evidence that ARM-01 needs a second arm, wheels or a learned policy.
