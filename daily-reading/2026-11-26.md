## What to borrow: score the end of the task

RoboDyna's authors report progress and success separately on simulated manipulation with physics-driven object motion. Their results show that partial progress can coexist with few completed trials. As older background, [Zheng and colleagues' September 25 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.31606v1#S7.E) gives a concrete hardware example: their Crazyflie sometimes struck the ball as intended, but did not complete target delivery. Those outcomes are specific to the papers' setups. The useful inference for BenchSort is that a bin crossing should not count as a completed sort.

## Practical takeaway: write the bounce-out counterexample first

For a later **synthetic, offline** evaluator fixture, hand-write two time-stamped traces with the same object, target bin, confirmed grasp and release. In both, the object crosses into the right bin. In one, it stays within the bin and below a predeclared speed threshold for the final dwell. In the other, it bounces out before the dwell ends. The independent observation stream should score the first as a pass, the second as a failure, and a trace missing enough observations to establish the dwell as unscorable. Keep those labels out of the controller input. A small state machine is enough to test the rule; there is no reason to adopt either research stack for it.
