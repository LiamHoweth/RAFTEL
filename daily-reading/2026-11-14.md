## Read first: keep the input and outcome separate

Our [offline observation-replay contract](../arm-01/docs/offline-observation-replay.md) is a useful short technical read. Its committed synthetic example has four presented objects, three detected identities and only one currently accepted observation. Missing detections and rejected observations stay visible. An accepted observation does not become an attempted pick or a successful sort.

This connects directly to the [verification plan](../arm-01/docs/verification-plan.md): physical grasp retention, transport and correct-bin release need their own observed outcomes. Even an eventual 90 successes in 100 trials is an observed rate, with uncertainty, rather than proof of a guaranteed 90% success probability.

## What other teams are doing

### Cerulion 2.0.0: replay what the program actually consumed

**Released November 12 at 14:40 UTC.** The official release describes verification that supplies native free-running nodes with their recorded input frames and explicitly refuses unsupported reads. Python nodes join its recording/replay workflow. Its ROS 2 replay is narrower: one process using single-threaded executors, subject to documented assumptions. [Release](https://github.com/cerulion-inc/cerulion/releases/tag/v2.0.0) and [versioned replay documentation](https://github.com/cerulion-inc/cerulion/blob/v2.0.0/docs/ros2_replay.md).

**RAFTEL relevance:** the useful idea is to preserve consumed inputs and report the first justified divergence instead of calling a vaguely similar rerun reproducible. ARM-01 already has explicit clock/frame/provenance boundaries; read this as a comparison, not a reason to replace them.

**Important limit:** the vendor's ROS replay publishes outputs live and can reach controllers or devices. It is not automatically a harmless simulation. The release also enables usage telemetry by default. No installation, pairing or execution is recommended here, and no RAFTEL integration was tested.

### MERL TacSushi: inspect how “success” was scored

**Report dated November 13.** The authors report 600 real-robot evaluation rollouts across methods and tasks, with 20 trials per method–task cell. Their full model averages **68.3%** on three in-distribution food tasks and **37.5%** on two held-out ingredients. Success uses a composite terminal visual-quality score from five human and three vision-language-model ratings. [MERL TR2026-173](https://www.merl.com/publications/docs/TR2026-173.pdf), sections 3–4.

These are author-reported experiments; they were not independently reproduced for this briefing. The report acknowledges evaluator uncertainty and limited ingredient transfer. Its visual score does not establish calibrated contact safety, and 600 is the total across comparisons, not the full model's trial count.

**RAFTEL relevance:** borrow the explicit denominators, held-out conditions and limitations. BenchSort should score observable correct-bin outcomes and keep drops, missed objects and assistance visible, rather than importing a food-quality rating or treating a successful-looking image as proof of reliable manipulation.
