## Company reading: test the assembled system

NVIDIA's **November 13** tutorial uses a UR17G arm, Robotiq 2F-140 gripper and can in Isaac Sim 6.2. This is a newly selected article, not a November 17 launch. Asset profiles check authored properties, but the completed scene still needs motion, contact and task testing.

Its demonstration uses known object positions, scripted waypoints, no vision and limited collision checks. Liquid motion and can deformation are absent. The simulated gripper effort setting is not a calibrated physical gripping force. The authors explicitly limit the result to that setup. [Primary tutorial](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/simready-assets-robot-pick-and-place).

**My takeaway for RAFTEL:** a clean CAD import, valid frame graph or successful IK solve should be treated as one prerequisite, not as proof of a working pick-and-place system. Keep the Thor source, transforms, contact assumptions and controller behavior separately traceable. Do not adopt this tutorial's robot, effort settings or software requirements as ARM-01 specifications.

## Practical next question

Before a future physics task is called successful, can its report distinguish:

- asset/schema checks;
- articulation and contact behavior;
- full grasp, transport and release outcomes;
- comparison with measured hardware?

Our [roadmap](../docs/roadmap.md) already separates these gates. The immediate priorities remain the existing fabrication evidence and unresolved native CAD/frame questions in [nextSteps.md](../nextSteps.md). No new simulator installation, hardware motion, purchase or engineering implementation is proposed today.
