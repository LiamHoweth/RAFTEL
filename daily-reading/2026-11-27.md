## What to borrow

In their simulated pose-tracking study, the PolicyIK authors report 93.49% strict success for one attempt with checkpoint averaging and 98.92% with their full retry protocol. Those numbers are specific to their policy, arm and targets. What transfers is the reporting rule: give a first-attempt result and a fixed-budget result separately, so a retry improvement cannot hide a weak initial solve.

In industry, [XPENG's November 25 IRON announcement](https://www.xpeng.com/pressroom/news/01a212b0d830a1e838d68a02a2110174) names store-assistant training as its first planned setting, targeting Q1 2027, with public release targeted for Q2. That's a company plan, not evidence of retail or household reliability. For RAFTEL, the comparable decision is to define one job clearly enough to measure before broadening the scope.

## Practical takeaway

For each nominal pose in the target set, freeze the start configuration, residual thresholds and iteration budget before the run. If retries are allowed, predeclare their seeds and order. Report first-attempt passes, passes within budget, and unsolved targets out of the **same** denominator. Keep position and orientation residuals and the exact terminal status for each attempt. A local stall is evidence about that attempt, not proof that the pose is unreachable. Do not translate the numerical result into calibrated reach, collision clearance, or a physical success rate.
