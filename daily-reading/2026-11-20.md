## External signal: successful simulation can depend on unavailable information

**Company-reported examples, published November 19:** NVIDIA describes an agent-built cable-routing and USB-C insertion simulation using Isaac Lab, Newton and a Robotiq gripper. Its controller receives information directly from the simulation; future work includes a friction-held grasp and observations obtainable from real cameras and sensors. That is a useful stated boundary, not evidence of physical cable insertion.

The same article includes the pick-and-place walkthrough covered here on November 17. That example is not new research merely because it appears in a newer roundup. No NVIDIA tooling adoption or hardware change is warranted by this reading alone.

**Inference for ARM-01:** before comparing a future learned controller with the existing sorter, compare their inputs. Perfect object pose, contact state or object-held truth can make a simulated policy look capable while concealing the perception problem. Our architecture already keeps synthetic observations separate from real calibration; preserve that boundary.

## Practical takeaway: one observation audit, on paper

For one proposed BenchSort pick-and-release sequence, list:

- What each decision needs to know: object pose, held/not-held state, destination release.
- Where that information comes from: synthetic input, model prediction, or an independent measurement.
- Its coordinate frame, timestamp and uncertainty.
- What should happen if it is missing, stale or contradictory.

This is a proposed review exercise, not a new implementation or test result. The spatial contract leaves workstation, camera and physical TCP relationships unresolved. Do not fill those gaps with nominal transforms just to make a demo run.
