## Read first: three things that affect the next decision

### 1. Follow the load path before comparing motor numbers

**Read:** [our load model](../arm-01/docs/thor-load-analysis.md), [transmission study](../arm-01/docs/thor-transmission-study.md), and the [upstream wrist mapping](https://github.com/AngelLM/Thor-ROS/blob/e11a56b5aa0d89f0b68a3ea7bd084eab4a050952/ws_thor/src/thor_controller/src/thor_interface.cpp). The controller is pinned background source, not a newly tested hardware integration.

In the illustrative 50% whole-part PLA scenario, a hypothetical horizontal shoulder pose with 200 g produces about **5.83 N·m shoulder** and **2.34 N·m elbow** holding demand, including modeled self mass. These are joint demands, not motor shaft ratings. Exact wrist self-loads remain null because their moving subsets are unresolved; the report supplies scoped contribution bounds.

The [geared-motor manufacturer page](https://www.omc-stepperonline.com/nema-17-stepper-motor-bipolar-l-33mm-w-gear-ratio-5-1-planetary-gearbox-17hs13-0404s-pg5), an undated living reference checked today, separates bare-motor holding torque, reduction and gearbox limits. None alone is a moving/continuous-duty rating for our arm.

**Question to carry forward:** which wrist parts move with fold, roll, or both, and how do the transmissions carry those joint demands? Count mass where it is mounted: all three geared motors sit in the lower Art1 assembly, including the remote elbow drive.

### 2. Use the Kobra 3 Max profile to replace mass assumptions

**Read:** the [official printer specifications](https://us.anycubic3d.com/products/kobra-3-max) and our [fabrication record](../arm-01/hardware/fabrication.md). The manufacturer lists a **420 × 420 × 500 mm** build volume. Printer ownership and nominal capacity are now known; installed nozzle, actual filament, slicer settings and successful part-fit evidence are not.

Next, slice a selected original part using the actual machine/material profile and record orientation, supports, walls, estimated total mass and print time. This is a useful offline planning step. A bounding box fitting on the bed does not settle tolerances, layer strength or mounting safety.

**Important distinction:** our 50% effective whole-part solid fraction is a mass assumption. It is not an instruction to choose 50% infill in the slicer.

### 3. Compare material behavior, not just the lightest result

**Read:** [the material comparison](../arm-01/hardware/cad/thor-material-comparison.json) and [PLA](https://prusament.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/PLA_Prusament_TDS_2021_10_EN.pdf)/[PETG](https://prusament.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/PETG_Prusament_TDS_2021_10_EN.pdf) technical sheets, both version 1.1, updated February 16, 2022. These are background product data, not results from Liam's printer.

| Reference density scenario, same 50% fraction | Modeled robot mass including base |
|---|---:|
| PLA | 5.650 kg |
| PETG | 5.702 kg |
| ABS | 5.301 kg |

These scenarios identify exact reference products/editions, not your actual spools. The Prusament sheets illustrate a tradeoff: their PLA specimens have higher tested tensile modulus, while PETG has a higher reported heat-deflection temperature. Neither value qualifies our printed links or establishes a service-temperature limit.

**Takeaway:** select material and orientation using stiffness, creep, heat and fit evidence. Density alone cannot make that decision. ABS availability is recorded; its machine/profile and printed-part qualification remain open.

## A ten-minute practical exercise

In the project's installed Python environment, from `arm-01`:

```sh
python tools/thor_load_analysis.py --cad-inventory hardware/cad/thor-mounted-geometry.json --compare-materials
```

Then write down your filament brands and current Kobra profile. Pick one part for a slicer mass estimate; compare that estimate with the model's density/fraction assumption. Keep geometry, material, infill, orientation and measured results separate. No print, purchase or powered test was performed by this reading update.
