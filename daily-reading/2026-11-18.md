## What another company is doing

Genesis AI announced a **$500 million Series A at a $3.5 billion post-money valuation** on November 17. Its company-issued release describes an integrated loop spanning human demonstration capture, its GENE model, simulation and the Eno robot. It says initial deployment focuses on industrial settings, with consumer applications later. [Primary company release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/genesis-ai-raises-500m-series-a-at-3-5b-valuation-to-master-human-level-dexterity-in-general-purpose-robots-and-deploy-at-scale-302941249.html).

Those are the company's funding and strategy statements, not independent proof of household reliability or a price/performance benchmark for ARM-01. The release does not provide the comparable trial protocol needed to judge our sorting task.

**My inference for RAFTEL:** borrow the feedback loop, not the hardware complexity. A single parallel gripper and a tightly bounded sorting task can teach us where observations, models and outcomes disagree. A dexterous hand, humanoid body or larger AI model is not required to begin collecting that evidence.

## A useful question for the next review

For one future authorized grasp-and-release trial, could another person reconstruct all four stages?

- **Expected:** object, pose, intended destination and predeclared success rule.
- **Commanded:** the model/configuration revision and requested action.
- **Observed:** independent evidence of what actually happened.
- **Explained:** failure category and the next change, without overwriting the original result.

The [architecture](../arm-01/docs/architecture.md) already separates task logic, control, observations and evaluation. Keep that separation as hardware arrives. Today this is a review question, not a request to start physical testing, buy sensors or implement another framework.
