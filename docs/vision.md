# RAFTEL vision

RAFTEL aims to make affordable, general-purpose household robots that perform chores, assist people, and interact naturally with their owners. Manufacturing accessible humanoid robots is the long-term mission, not the claimed capability of ARM-01.

ARM-01 BenchSort focuses on useful physical manipulation before mobility. Its current mechanical study starts with existing Thor CAD, following Liam's allowance of up to eight actuators while preserving six independent arm axes plus a gripper. This study does not release a design for fabrication or purchase. A stationary arm provides a bounded environment for learning reliable observation, grasping, motion, placement, and recovery while collecting evidence that can transfer to later platforms.

## Engineering principles

- Treat affordability as a requirement that competes openly with reliability, capability, and safety.
- Prefer proven, reusable mechanical designs and open technologies when their licenses and technical evidence support the intended use.
- Design modular, repairable components with documented interfaces and replaceable wear parts.
- Keep perception, planning, task behavior, and hardware control independent.
- Validate assumptions through calculations, repeatable software tests, calibrated simulation, and supervised physical experiments.
- Record uncertainty and failures. Separate proposed designs, supplier claims, analytical results, simulation results, and measured physical results.
- Consider manufacturing, cable serviceability, part availability, and future mobile integration from the start.

## Scope boundaries

The present project is a removable stationary arm and supporting software. It does not include mobile-base construction, unattended household use, human contact tasks, or a product safety claim. Company-scale architecture and spending should follow demonstrated needs rather than speculative future features.
