## Comparison actuator evidence

[Feetech's C001 catalogue](https://www.feetechrc.com/Data/feetechrc/upload/file/20240706/2024%E9%A3%9E%E7%89%B9%E5%AE%A3%E4%BC%A0%E5%86%8C.pdf) identifies ST-3215-C001 as 55 g, rated 5 kg·cm and 650 mA at 7.4 V, versus stall 19.5 kg·cm and 2.5 A. These manufacturer ratings are **not** demonstrated ARM-01 continuous-duty capability. With 1 kg·cm = 0.0980665 N·m, rated torque is approximately 0.490 N·m and stall torque 1.912 N·m.

The [STS3215 A/0 datasheet](https://www.feetechrc.com/Data/feetechrc/upload/file/20200611/6372749961523760249976542.pdf) documents a TTL half-duplex interface and 4–7.4 V input; match the purchased suffix and revision rather than extrapolating across the product family. No 12 V supply may be inferred compatible with C001. The catalogue was found through indexed manufacturer text; direct catalogue fetch timed out in this session, while the eight-page datasheet was read successfully. Accessed 2026-11-11.

## Comparison budget alternatives and release gates

- Reuse a camera, mount, bins and fabrication material to reduce the allowance by roughly $50–90. This does not establish the $350 low-end target as feasible.
- Reserve an **additional $100–250 estimate** if shoulder/elbow need larger actuators, reduction, bearings or counterbalance. That shifts the above allowance to approximately $601–751 before tax/shipping. Final engineering may cost more.
- An existing heavier six-axis platform may improve mechanical confidence but exceed both mass and budget goals; compare whole-system cost, not servo cost alone.
- Do not purchase seven C001s merely to fit $500. Finalize CAD revision, measured downstream masses, working torque margin, thermal test method, electronics topology, mounting and license obligations first.
